Ask About a Consultation

What Is Breast Augmentation?

Breast augmentation, also called augmentation mammoplasty, is surgery to increase or restore breast volume and modify breast shape. It may be considered for naturally small breasts, volume loss after pregnancy or weight change, differences between the breasts, or a preference for greater fullness and projection.

At Bukit Tinggi Medical Centre in Klang, Dr Agnes Yeo assesses each patient individually. The consultation considers breast width, tissue thickness, skin quality, nipple and breast-fold position, chest-wall anatomy and the patient’s goals before discussing suitable options.

  • Individual assessment for implants or fat grafting
  • Planning based on body proportions and tissue characteristics
  • Specific assessment of breast and nipple asymmetry
  • Clear discussion of expected changes, limitations and risks

Breast Augmentation Options

Breast Implants

Implants can provide a larger and more predictable increase in volume than fat grafting. Selection involves implant width, projection, volume and shape, as well as the amount of natural tissue available to cover the implant.

  • Suitable for a wider range of volume changes
  • Multiple widths, volumes and projection profiles
  • Placement and incision planned for the individual
  • Requires long-term implant monitoring

Fat Grafting to the Breast

Fat grafting transfers a patient’s own fat from another area of the body to the breasts. It may suit selected patients seeking a modest volume increase, contour refinement or correction of a localised difference.

  • Uses the patient’s own tissue
  • Can provide modest, targeted volume enhancement
  • Requires suitable donor fat
  • Some transferred fat may not persist, so results vary

Breast Asymmetry and Uneven Breasts

Some difference between the breasts is common. Asymmetry may involve breast volume, width, shape, nipple height, the level of the inframammary fold beneath the breast, or the chest wall itself.

Using identical implants does not always create the closest possible match. Depending on the cause and degree of asymmetry, planning may include different implant volumes or profiles, selective fat grafting, adjustment of a breast fold, or another breast procedure. Implant surgery alone cannot correct every difference, particularly a marked difference in nipple position or breast droop.

What Is Assessed?

  • Differences in breast volume and base width
  • Nipple and areola position
  • Breast-fold height
  • Skin quality, tissue thickness and breast droop
  • Chest-wall and rib-cage differences

The aim is improvement, not guaranteed perfect symmetry.

What Breast Augmentation May Address

Breast augmentation may increase naturally small breast volume, restore some fullness lost after pregnancy or weight change, and modify breast projection in proportion to the patient’s frame.

It may also improve selected differences in breast volume or shape. The appropriate approach depends on the underlying anatomy rather than the concern alone.

Body Proportion

Adjust breast width, fullness or projection in a way that is planned around the patient’s frame and preferences.

Important Limitation

An implant adds volume, but it cannot correct every degree of breast droop, low nipple position or structural asymmetry. A breast lift or another procedure may sometimes be more suitable.

Selected Asymmetry

Improve certain volume or shape differences, while recognising that another procedure may be needed when droop or nipple position differs significantly.

Early Recovery

Recovery varies between patients and procedures. Light daily activity may be possible during the early recovery period, while driving, lifting and strenuous exercise may need to be restricted according to personalised advice.

4

Follow-up and Settling

Swelling and tightness generally reduce over time. Breast shape and implant position continue to settle over the following weeks and months, with follow-up arranged according to clinical need.

Risks and Important Considerations

Breast augmentation is surgery and results cannot be guaranteed. General surgical risks include bleeding, infection, wound-healing problems, scarring and anaesthetic complications.

Implant-related considerations include capsular contracture, implant rupture, implant movement or rotation, visible rippling, asymmetry, altered nipple or breast sensation and the possibility of revision surgery. Rare implant-associated conditions and the monitoring recommended for the chosen device should also be discussed.

Breast Implants Are Not Lifetime Devices

Future assessment or surgery may be required because of implant changes, ageing, pregnancy, weight change, complications or a change in personal preference. Your consultation should include the expected follow-up for the implant selected.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the right breast implant size selected?

Implant selection is based on more than a desired cup size. Breast base width, existing tissue, chest-wall shape, skin quality, asymmetry, implant diameter and projection all need to be considered during consultation.

Can I breastfeed after breast augmentation?

Many patients can breastfeed after breast augmentation, but no operation can guarantee future breastfeeding. Surgical technique, individual anatomy and other factors can affect milk production, so future pregnancy plans should be discussed during consultation.

Can breast augmentation improve uneven breasts?

It may improve some volume and shape differences. Depending on the anatomy, planning may involve different implant sizes or profiles, fat grafting, fold adjustment or another procedure. Perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed.

Can I still have mammograms with breast implants?

Breast screening remains important after augmentation. Tell the imaging centre that you have implants so the team can use appropriate views and techniques. Follow the screening advice recommended for your age, risk factors and implant type.

What is recovery after breast augmentation like?

Recovery varies. Many patients can manage light daily activity during the early recovery period, but lifting, strenuous exercise and driving may need to be restricted. Swelling settles gradually and the breasts continue to soften over the following weeks and months.

Should I wait until after pregnancy to have breast augmentation?

Pregnancy and breastfeeding can change breast volume, skin and shape, which may alter an augmentation result. The timing is personal and should be discussed in relation to pregnancy plans, health and expectations.

What are the risks of breast augmentation?

Possible risks include bleeding, infection, scarring, altered sensation, implant malposition, asymmetry, capsular contracture, implant rupture and the need for revision surgery. The risks relevant to you should be discussed during consultation.

Do I need a breast lift instead of implants?

Implants add volume but do not reliably correct every degree of breast droop or low nipple position. Some patients may benefit from a breast lift, with or without augmentation. Examination is needed to explain the trade-offs and scars involved.

Are breast implants permanent?

No. Breast implants are not lifetime devices. Even when there is no immediate problem, future monitoring and additional surgery may be required because of implant changes, ageing, pregnancy, weight change or personal preference.
