Lump Under Skin: What Does It Mean?

A lump under the skin is a common concern. Most are harmless, such as lipoma or cysts, but some may require medical evaluation—especially if they grow, become painful, or change in appearance. If you’re in Klang, early assessment helps determine whether it is safe to observe or should be removed.

What Could a Lump Be?

  • Lipoma: Soft, movable, usually painless
  • Epidermoid cyst: May have a central punctum, can become infected
  • Abscess: Painful, red, warm swelling due to infection
  • Benign growth: Firm but non-cancerous
  • Suspicious lump: Irregular, hard, fast-growing

Not all lumps are dangerous—but not all are safe either.

When Should You Worry?

  • Growing quickly
  • Hard or fixed in place
  • Painful
  • Skin changes (ulcer, redness)
  • Bleeding or discharge
  • Persists beyond a few weeks

These signs do not always mean cancer—but they should not be ignored.

Do All Lumps Need Removal?

You may not need removal if:

  • Small and stable
  • No symptoms

You may consider removal if:

  • Increasing in size
  • Causing discomfort
  • Cosmetic concern
  • Uncertain diagnosis

How Are Lumps Treated?

  • Observation: For harmless lumps
  • Medication: If infection is present
  • Minor surgery: Removal under local anaesthesia

Most procedures are quick, done as a day procedure, and require minimal downtime.

Recovery and Expectations

  • Same-day discharge
  • Small wound with stitches
  • Mild discomfort for a few days
  • Low complication risk

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a lump always cancer?

No. Most lumps are benign, such as lipoma or cysts.

How do I know if my lump is dangerous?

Warning signs include rapid growth, pain, hardness, or skin changes.

Can I leave a lump untreated?

Yes, if confirmed benign and not causing issues—but monitoring is important.

Is lump removal painful?

It is usually done under local anaesthesia with minimal discomfort.

Will it leave a scar?

A small scar is expected, but techniques aim to minimise visibility.
